# Apple Pie

## Description

This comprehensive recipe guides you through making a homemade apple pie with a flaky crust and delicious apple filling, perfect for impressing family and friends. Don't forget to serve it with vanilla ice cream!

**Tags:** apple, pie, dessert, baking, fruit

## Ingredients

- 1 cup butter-flavored shortening
- 2 ½ cups flour
- ½ teaspoon salt
- 1 egg
- ½ cup cold water (use ice if necessary)
- 1 tablespoon apple cider vinegar
- 6-8 high-quality apples (Gala, Granny Smith, Braeburn recommended, or a mixture)
- ¾ cup sugar
- ½ teaspoon cinnamon
- 1/8 teaspoon nutmeg
- 3 tablespoons cornstarch
- 1 cup unfiltered apple cider (or Just Apple Juice)
- 2 tablespoons bottled lemon juice
- whole milk
- coarse/turbinado sugar

## Steps

1. Stir the flour and salt together.
2. Gently blend the shortening into the flour mixture with your hands or a pastry cutter until crumbly and chunky. Set this mixture aside.
3. Beat the egg and cold water together. Add the apple cider vinegar to the egg mixture.
4. Pour the liquid mixture into the flour mixture and lightly fold them together. Let it sit for a few minutes to let the water start to absorb.
5. Using your hands again, mix until a dough forms. If it is still too crumbly, add an additional 1-3 tablespoons of cold water, being careful not to overmix.
6. Score the dough down the middle with the edge of your hand and pull it apart into two pieces.
7. Shape the dough pieces into two round discs, wrap them in plastic wrap, and then chill them for one hour in the refrigerator (or half an hour in the freezer).
8. While the dough is chilling, make the apple filling sauce: In a heavy saucepan, whisk together the sugar, cinnamon, nutmeg, and cornstarch.
9. Stir in the apple cider and lemon juice. Cook on medium heat until thick and bubbly.
10. Scoop the sauce into a bowl and chill in the refrigerator.
11. Prepare the apples: Peel, core, and slice the apples into about ¼ inch slices and place them in a microwave-safe bowl.
12. Cover the bowl with plastic wrap and microwave for 2 minutes. Then stir the apples and microwave for another 1-2 minutes until warm and starting to soften.
13. Stir the cooled sauce into the apples and place back in the refrigerator.
14. To make the bottom pie crust, take one of the cooled dough disks out of the fridge. On a floured surface, roll the dough from the center out into a circle until about 3/8 of an inch thick, ensuring it is 1-1.5 inches larger than your pie pan.
15. Carefully fold the dough in half and then in fourths to pick it up and transfer it to your pie pan. Press the dough into the pan to remove any air pockets.
16. Put the bottom crust in the freezer for 10-15 minutes to get it cold again.
17. Pour the apple filling into the bottom pie crust. Carefully move apple slices around until all the sides are filled and the top is even.
18. Put the pie back in the refrigerator while you roll out the other dough disk for the top crust.
19. Decide on your top crust style: For a traditional top crust, lay the rolled out dough on top of your pie. Trim any excess dough, roll the two crusts together (bottom side up), and then crimp the edges. Cut slits in the top crust to allow air to escape. For a lattice top crust, roll the dough out, cut into 1-inch strips with a pizza cutter, then alternate laying the strips down in a lattice pattern. Trim the strips to the length of the bottom crust and roll the bottom crust up, tucking the strips in as you go. Crimp the edges.
20. Brush the top crust lightly with whole milk (avoiding crimped edges for traditional) and sprinkle with coarse/turbinado sugar.
21. Bake at 400 degrees Fahrenheit for 20 minutes.
22. Reduce the heat to 350 degrees Fahrenheit (leaving the pie in the oven) and bake for another 30-40 minutes until the crust is golden brown.
23. Allow the pie to cool and rest for at least an hour or two before cutting to allow the juices to thicken.

## Notes

When choosing apples, pick varieties like Gala, Granny Smith, or Braeburn that feel heavy and are firm. A mixture of varieties adds sweet and tart flavors. Avoid overmixing pie crust dough as it can make it tough; look for marbling from the shortening for a flaky crust. If making a pie with only a bottom crust, shape edges and poke holes in the bottom to prevent bubbling. There are many ways to crimp edges; using your thumb and pointer finger to pinch or pressing with a fork are easy methods. If an edge is not thick enough, tuck excess dough inside before rolling over. If you run out of strips for a lattice, trim excess length from used ones and pinch them together to make more. This pie is best served with vanilla ice cream.
