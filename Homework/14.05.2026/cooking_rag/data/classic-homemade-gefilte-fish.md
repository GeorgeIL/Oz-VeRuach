# Classic Homemade Gefilte Fish

## Description

This traditional Jewish dish features finely ground white fish patties gently poached in a savory broth, often served chilled with a carrot slice and horseradish.

**Tags:** jewish, traditional, fish, appetizer, holiday

## Ingredients

- 2 lbs mixed white fish fillets (such as carp, pike, whitefish, cod), skinned and deboned
- 2 large onions, quartered
- 2 large eggs
- 1/2 cup matzo meal (or plain breadcrumbs)
- 1/4 cup cold water (or fish broth)
- 2 tablespoons sugar (adjust to taste for sweetness)
- 1 teaspoon salt (or to taste)
- 1/2 teaspoon freshly ground black pepper (or to taste)
- Fish bones, heads, and skins from the fish fillets (optional, but highly recommended for flavor)
- 2 large carrots, peeled and sliced into 1/4-inch rounds
- 1 large onion, peeled and quartered
- 1 celery stalk, roughly chopped
- 8-10 cups cold water (enough to cover the fish patties)
- 1 tablespoon salt
- 1 teaspoon sugar
- 1/2 teaspoon black peppercorns

## Steps

1. Cut the fish fillets into smaller chunks. In a food processor, pulse the fish chunks and quartered onions until finely ground but not pasty. Alternatively, you can use a meat grinder. Transfer the ground fish and onion mixture to a large bowl. Add the eggs, matzo meal, 1/4 cup cold water, 2 tablespoons sugar, 1 teaspoon salt, and 1/2 teaspoon black pepper. Mix thoroughly with your hands until well combined. The mixture should be firm enough to shape. If too wet, add a little more matzo meal; if too dry, add a tiny bit more water. Taste a tiny bit of the raw mixture (or cook a small spoonful in the microwave for 30 seconds) and adjust seasonings.
2. In a large, wide pot or Dutch oven, arrange the fish bones, heads, and skins (if using) at the bottom. Scatter the sliced carrots, quartered onion, and chopped celery over the fish bones. Add the 8-10 cups of cold water, 1 tablespoon salt, 1 teaspoon sugar, and black peppercorns. Bring to a gentle simmer over medium heat.
3. With wet hands, shape the fish mixture into oval or round patties, about 2-3 inches long and 1 inch thick. Carefully place the shaped gefilte fish patties into the simmering broth. Ensure they are submerged. You may need to do this in batches if your pot isn't large enough, or use two pots. Once all patties are in, bring the broth back to a very gentle simmer. Reduce heat to low, cover the pot, and cook for 1.5 to 2 hours. The fish should be tender and cooked through.
4. Once cooked, carefully remove the gefilte fish patties from the broth using a slotted spoon and arrange them on a platter. Strain the poaching broth, discarding the solids (bones, onions, celery, peppercorns), but reserve the cooked carrot slices. Pour some of the strained broth over the fish patties. Let the fish cool completely to room temperature, then cover and refrigerate for at least 4 hours, or preferably overnight, to allow the flavors to meld and the broth to gel.
5. Serve the chilled gefilte fish patties garnished with a reserved carrot slice on top of each. It's traditionally served with a side of red or white horseradish (chrain).

## Notes

For a firmer texture, some recipes suggest refrigerating the fish mixture for 30 minutes before shaping.
The sweetness of gefilte fish is a matter of regional preference; adjust the sugar content to your liking. Some prefer a very sweet fish, while others like it savory.
The poaching broth will often set into a delicate, flavorful jelly (aspic) when chilled, which is highly prized.
Gefilte fish can be made several days in advance and stored in the refrigerator, submerged in its poaching liquid.
