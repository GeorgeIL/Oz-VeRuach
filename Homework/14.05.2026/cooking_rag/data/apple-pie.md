# Apple Pie

## Description

A comprehensive recipe for a classic apple pie, featuring a homemade, flaky crust and a spiced apple filling, with detailed steps for preparation, assembly, and baking.

**Tags:** apple pie, dessert, baking, fruit pie, homemade

## Ingredients

- 1 cup butter-flavored shortening
- 2 ½ cups flour
- ½ teaspoon salt
- 1 egg
- ½ cup cold water (use ice if necessary)
- 1 tablespoon apple cider vinegar
- 6-8 high-quality apples
- ¾ cup sugar
- ½ teaspoon cinnamon
- 1/8 teaspoon nutmeg
- 3 tablespoons cornstarch
- 1 cup unfiltered apple cider
- 2 tablespoons bottled lemon juice
- whole milk
- coarse/turbinado sugar

## Steps

1. Stir the flour and salt together.
2. Gently blend the shortening into the flour mixture with your hands or a pastry cutter until crumbly and chunky. Set this mixture aside.
3. Beat the egg and cold water together. Add the apple cider vinegar to the egg mixture.
4. Pour the liquid mixture into the flour mixture and lightly fold them together. Let it sit for a few minutes to let the water start to absorb.
5. Using your hands, mix until a dough forms. If still too crumbly, add an additional 1-3 tablespoons of cold water, being careful not to over mix.
6. Score the dough down the middle with the edge of your hand and pull it apart into two pieces. Shape them into two round discs, wrap in plastic wrap, and chill for one hour in the refrigerator (or 30 minutes in the freezer).
7. While the dough is chilling, make the apple filling: in a heavy saucepan, whisk together the sugar, cinnamon, nutmeg, and cornstarch. Stir in the apple cider and lemon juice.
8. Cook on medium heat until thick and bubbly. When finished, scoop the sauce into a bowl and chill in the refrigerator.
9. Begin preparing the apples: peel, core, and slice them into about ¼ inch slices and place them in a microwave safe bowl.
10. Cover the bowl with plastic wrap and microwave for 2 minutes. Then stir the apples and microwave for another 1-2 minutes until warm and starting to soften.
11. Stir in the cooled sauce and place back in the refrigerator.
12. To make the bottom pie crust: take one cooled dough disk out of the fridge. On a floured surface, roll the dough from the center out into a circle about 3/8 inch thick, ensuring it's 1-1.5 inches larger than your pie pan all around.
13. Carefully fold the dough in half and then in fourths to pick it up and transfer it to your pie pan. Press the dough into the pan to remove any air pockets.
14. Put the bottom crust in the freezer for 10-15 minutes to get it cold again.
15. Pour the apple filling into the bottom pie crust. Carefully move apple slices around until all the sides are filled and the top is even.
16. Put the pie back in the refrigerator while you roll out the other dough disk.
17. Decide what style top you want to make: For a traditional top crust, lay the rolled out dough on top of your pie. Trim any excess dough to just even it up, leaving as much length as you can. Roll the two crusts together, bottom side up all the way around, then crimp the edges. Cut slits in the top crust to allow air to escape. Brush it lightly with whole milk, avoiding the crimped edges, and sprinkle with coarse sugar. OR For a lattice top crust, roll the dough out, cut into 1-inch strips with a pizza cutter, then alternate laying the strips down in a lattice pattern. Trim the strips to the length of the bottom crust and roll the bottom crust up, tucking the strips in as you go. Crimp the edges. Gently rub milk onto the top with your fingers and sprinkle with coarse sugar.
18. Bake at 400 degrees Fahrenheit for 20 minutes, then (leaving the pie in the oven) reduce the heat to 350 degrees Fahrenheit and bake for another 30-40 minutes until the crust is golden brown.
19. Allow the pie to cool and rest for at least an hour or two before cutting to allow the juices to thicken.
20. Serve with vanilla ice cream.

## Notes

Choose heavy, firm apples (like Gala, Granny Smith, or Braeburn), often a mixture works well for balanced sweet/tart flavors. When making the pie crust, avoid overmixing or adding too much water; aim for visible shortening marbling for a flaky crust. If making a bottom-only crust, poke holes in the bottom to prevent bubbling. Allow the pie to cool for 1-2 hours before slicing to allow the juices to thicken.
