# Classic Russian Borscht

## Description

A vibrant, hearty, and comforting beet soup, rich with vegetables and a touch of tang, perfect for a cold day.

**Tags:** soup, russian, hearty, vegetarian-option, winter-warmer

## Ingredients

- 2 tablespoons olive oil
- 1 large onion, chopped
- 2 carrots, peeled and shredded or finely diced
- 3-4 medium beets, peeled and shredded or diced
- 1/2 head cabbage (about 4-5 cups), thinly shredded
- 2-3 medium potatoes, peeled and diced
- 2 cloves garlic, minced
- 1 (14.5 ounce) can diced tomatoes, or 3 tablespoons tomato paste
- 6-8 cups vegetable or beef broth
- 1/4 cup apple cider vinegar or white vinegar (or to taste)
- 1 teaspoon sugar (optional, to balance tartness)
- Salt and freshly ground black pepper to taste
- Fresh dill, chopped, for garnish
- Sour cream or Greek yogurt, for serving

## Steps

1. In a large pot or Dutch oven, heat the olive oil over medium heat. Add the chopped onion and carrots, and sauté for 5-7 minutes until softened.
2. Stir in the shredded beets and cabbage. Cook for another 5-10 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the cabbage begins to wilt.
3. Add the diced potatoes and minced garlic, cooking for 1 minute until fragrant. Stir in the diced tomatoes (or tomato paste).
4. Pour in the vegetable or beef broth. Bring the soup to a boil, then reduce the heat to low, cover, and simmer for 30-40 minutes, or until the beets and potatoes are tender.
5. Stir in the apple cider vinegar (or white vinegar) and optional sugar. Season generously with salt and freshly ground black pepper. Taste and adjust seasonings as needed – you might want more vinegar for tang or a pinch more sugar to balance.
6. Ladle the hot borscht into bowls. Garnish each serving with a generous dollop of sour cream or Greek yogurt and a sprinkle of fresh chopped dill.

## Notes

For an even richer flavor, you can roast the beets before adding them to the soup. Simply toss diced beets with a little olive oil, salt, and pepper, then roast at 400°F (200°C) for 20-30 minutes until tender.
Borscht often tastes even better the next day as the flavors have more time to meld together.
Feel free to adjust the amount of vinegar and sugar to your personal preference for sweetness and tang.
